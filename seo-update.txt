# AIVIZED.COM — H1, Meta Title and Meta Description Alignment Rule

## THE RULE IN ONE SENTENCE

Every page must have its H1 keyword reflected inside both the meta title and the meta description on that same page. All three must be topically aligned.

---

## WHAT THIS MEANS PRACTICALLY

The H1 is the primary topic signal for a page. The meta title and meta description must reinforce that same topic. If the H1 says one thing and the meta title says something different, Google sees a mismatch and it hurts the ranking for both.

This does not mean copy the H1 text word for word into the title and description. It means the core keyword phrase from the H1 must appear in both.

---

## CORRECT EXAMPLE

H1: Restaurant AI chatbot that handles reservations and enquiries 24/7

Core keyword extracted from H1: "restaurant AI chatbot"

Meta title: Restaurant AI Chatbot for UK Businesses | AIVized
(contains "restaurant AI chatbot" from the H1 ✓)

Meta description: AIVized installs a managed AI chatbot for UK restaurants. Handles reservations and menu questions 24/7. No technical setup from you. Plans from £29/month.
(contains "AI chatbot" and "restaurants" mirroring the H1 topic ✓)

---

## WRONG EXAMPLE

H1: Restaurant AI chatbot that handles reservations and enquiries 24/7

Meta title: Managed Chatbot Service for Hospitality | AIVized
(keyword mismatch, "hospitality" is not what the H1 is about ✗)

Meta description: AIVized helps businesses automate their customer communication with smart AI tools.
(generic, does not reflect the H1 topic at all ✗)

---

## RULE FOR CLAUDE CODE

For every page on aivized.com, follow this three step process:

Step 1: Write the H1 first. Identify the core keyword phrase in that H1.

Step 2: Write the meta title. It must contain the core keyword phrase from step 1. Keep it between 50 and 60 characters.

Step 3: Write the meta description. It must contain the same core keyword phrase from step 1. Keep it between 150 and 160 characters.

If the meta title or meta description does not contain the H1 keyword, rewrite it until it does.

---

## APPLY TO ALL PAGE TYPES

This rule applies to every page without exception:
- Homepage
- All 7 industry pages
- All 70 city pages
- All blog posts
- Pricing page
- FAQ page
- About page
- Contact page

---

## VALIDATION CHECK

After writing H1, meta title, and meta description for any page, ask:

"Does the core keyword from the H1 appear in the meta title?" — If no, rewrite the meta title.
"Does the core keyword from the H1 appear in the meta description?" — If no, rewrite the meta description.

Both answers must be yes before moving to the next page.
